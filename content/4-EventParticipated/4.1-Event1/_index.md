## Summary of Content and Lessons Learned

### 1. The Iceberg of Procrastination: When "Laziness" Is Just the Tip of Fear

#### Speaker Information

This topic was presented by **Khac Uy Pham**, a third-year student at Vietnamese-German University.

His talk centered on the habit of procrastination and how it affects our studies, work, and personal lives.

#### Main Content

The key idea of the session was that procrastination should not simply be labeled as "laziness." In the speaker's view, laziness is only the visible part of the iceberg. Hidden underneath are often deeper fears, such as:

- The fear of not being good enough.
- The fear of being judged by others.
- The fear of making mistakes.
- The fear of failing.

The speaker then walked us through the negative cycle of procrastination. Whenever we face a difficult task, we tend to escape into social media, games, or other distractions to feel better for a short while.

But after that, guilt, stress, and self-criticism start to build up. As a result, starting the original task becomes even harder, and the procrastination loop keeps repeating itself.

Besides explaining the causes, the speaker shared several practical ways to overcome it, such as:

- Clearly identifying and naming our fears.
- Using the five-minute rule.
- Splitting big tasks into smaller, simpler ones.
- Starting with very small actions.
- Not waiting until we feel fully confident before acting.

A strong point he made was that action comes before confidence. Instead of waiting for the perfect moment or enough courage, we should take the first step to build momentum and keep going.

#### Lessons Learned

The biggest lesson for me was to see procrastination for what it really is. Instead of blaming ourselves for being lazy, we should ask:

> "What am I afraid of?"

Once we name the fear, it becomes much easier to find the right way to deal with it.

I also learned that there is no such thing as a "perfect moment" to start. Breaking work into small pieces and completing just the first tiny step can create the motivation needed to keep moving forward.

In studying and working, especially in tech, the **"Go build"** mindset matters a lot: just start, try things, fail fast, learn quickly, and keep improving.

### 2. Why We Always Need Confidence

#### Speaker Information

The speaker for this topic was **Nguyen Thi Quynh Nhu**.

Her talk focused on the role of confidence in learning, student life, career growth, and social relationships.

#### Main Content

The session began by clarifying what confidence really means.

According to the speaker, being confident does not mean:

- Having a big ego.
- Being perfect all the time.
- Knowing everything.
- Always having the right answers.

Instead, confidence is the willingness to try, to act, and to express ourselves even when we feel anxious or uncertain.

She also explained how a lack of confidence can hold students back. Self-doubt often makes us:

- Miss valuable opportunities.
- Hide our true abilities.
- Avoid sharing our ideas.
- Fear speaking in public.
- Put invisible pressure on ourselves.

Many people are actually knowledgeable and skilled, but they do not dare to show it, defend their ideas, or grab opportunities.

The speaker also touched on the psychological reasons behind low confidence, including:

- Impostor syndrome.
- The Dunning-Kruger effect.
- Fear of being judged.
- Constantly comparing ourselves with others.

Her message was that technical skills are the foundation, but confidence is the bridge that lets us communicate, collaborate, defend our ideas, and own our decisions.

She also gave simple ways to become more confident every day, for example:

- Preparing well before presentations, interviews, or projects.
- Acknowledging small wins and personal progress.
- Taking part in group activities.
- Using the five-second rule to overcome hesitation.
- Not being afraid to make mistakes and learning from them.

#### Lessons Learned

What I took away is that confidence is not something we are simply born with. It is a skill that we can practice and grow over time.

To become more confident, we should start with small actions, prepare better, and recognize our own progress.

The session also reminded me that knowledge alone is not enough. In both study and work, confidence helps us:

- Present ideas more clearly.
- Communicate more effectively.
- Build stronger connections.
- Actively seize opportunities.
- Grow both personally and professionally.

So confidence is one of the most important keys that allows us to fully show and develop our abilities.

### 3. The Hidden Iceberg of a Project: DevOps Before Disaster

#### Speaker Information

This topic was presented by **Tran Minh Quan**.

His talk explored the hidden problems inside a software project and how DevOps can help prevent risks before incidents happen.

#### Main Content

The speaker used the iceberg model to explain common issues in software projects.

The visible part of the iceberg stands for the symptoms we can easily observe, such as:

- Missed deadlines.
- Errors appearing in production.
- Failed deployments.
- Complaints from customers.
- Overworked and exhausted team members.

But the real causes lie beneath the surface, including:

- Unclear project requirements.
- Poor communication among team members.
- Teams working in silos.
- A lack of ownership and accountability.
- Manual working processes.
- Slow feedback loops.
- Weak coordination between development and operations.

The speaker stressed that fixing only the visible symptoms is not enough. If a team merely reacts to errors, constantly "fights fires," and patches surface issues, the same problems will keep coming back.

Instead, teams should apply systems thinking to find and solve the root causes.

The core of his talk was the real meaning of DevOps. DevOps is not just about tools like:

- Docker.
- Kubernetes.
- CI/CD.
- Cloud.
- Terraform.

DevOps is actually a combination of three key elements:

1. **People**
2. **Processes**
3. **Technology**

His point was that we can automate deployment, but we cannot automate trust, communication, and a sense of ownership among people.

He also introduced several important DevOps principles, such as:

- Encouraging collaboration to break down barriers between teams.
- Using automation to reduce manual mistakes.
- Creating fast feedback loops to catch problems early.
- Continuously improving to avoid repeating mistakes.
- Sharing responsibility across the whole product lifecycle.

#### Lessons Learned

The most important lesson for me was that the visible problems of a project are usually just the outcomes of deeper root causes.

To keep a project healthy, the team must actively look for and fix root causes, instead of only patching errors after they appear.

The session also helped me see that DevOps is not just for technical engineers. It is about how the whole team works together.

Clear communication, shared responsibility, automation, and fast feedback are all factors that help a project avoid major risks.

For anyone studying or working in tech, this is a valuable lesson about building a workflow that is sustainable, efficient, and always improving.

### 4. Level Up Your AWS Skills with Cloud Quest and Floci

#### Speaker Information

This topic was presented by **Huynh Thai Linh**.

His talk focused on how to improve AWS skills using AWS Cloud Quest and the open-source tool Floci.

#### Main Content

The speaker started by describing the common struggles of beginners who learn AWS through hands-on practice.

Many learners face problems such as:

- Worrying about the cost of AWS services.
- Constantly checking their bills.
- Fearing they will forget to delete created resources.
- Being worried about unexpected charges.
- Not knowing which service to start with.

To solve these problems, he introduced **AWS Cloud Quest** as a free, beginner-friendly practice environment.

AWS Cloud Quest lets learners explore AWS through game-based missions, which makes learning cloud architecture and services more visual and engaging.

Next, he introduced **Floci**, an open-source tool that emulates AWS services locally.

Floci helps learners and developers:

- Test architectures faster.
- Write and test code on their own computers.
- Reduce cloud usage costs.
- Avoid unexpected charges.
- Speed up development and testing.

The speaker also compared Floci with LocalStack based on:

- Speed.
- Performance.
- Storage usage.
- AWS service support.
- Cost.

However, he also mentioned the limitations of Floci:

- It does not support every AWS service.
- Some services only return simulated results.
- Local results may differ from the real cloud environment.
- It cannot fully replace testing on real AWS.

Finally, he proposed a three-stage AWS learning path:

1. **Stage 1:** Use AWS Cloud Quest to understand cloud concepts and architecture.
2. **Stage 2:** Use Floci to write code and test quickly in a local environment.
3. **Stage 3:** Deploy on real AWS to experience an actual environment.

#### Lessons Learned

The key takeaway was that learning AWS needs a proper path to reduce pressure on beginners.

Instead of jumping straight to the cloud and constantly worrying about cost, learners can start with simulated or game-based environments to build a solid foundation first.

It is also important to combine different tools during the learning process:

- AWS Cloud Quest builds architectural thinking.
- Floci allows fast, low-cost testing.
- Real AWS gives experience with actual deployment.

Still, we should understand the limits of each tool and not rely completely on simulated environments.
