## Summary of Content and Lessons Learned

### 1. Cloud Trends, IT Recruitment Market and Career Approach

#### Speaker Information

The session was opened by **Anh Gia Hung**, who presented a perspective on the IT recruitment market and how to approach a career in the technology industry.

The presentation focused on the current trends in cloud computing, digital transformation, and the job opportunities available to students and new graduates.

#### Main Content

The presentation began by analyzing the **Cloud First strategy**, in which businesses prioritize cloud-based solutions when planning their digital transformation.

According to the speaker, the scale of the cloud computing market is growing rapidly compared to traditional hardware, creating many job opportunities at **Talent Hubs** in Vietnam.

Cloud applications were also introduced in important industry fields, including:

- Core Banking systems.
- Fintech and financial services.
- Data analytics to detect fraudulent transactions.
- Enterprise cloud architectures.

The speaker then discussed the importance of choosing a career direction:

- Choosing an industry domain such as banking, fintech, e-commerce, or logistics.
- Choosing a professional role such as DevOps, Data, or AI.
- Combining domain knowledge with technical expertise.

Another important message was about building a capability profile through:

- Real side projects that solve actual business problems.
- Participation in technology communities (networking).
- Publicly sharing work on platforms such as GitHub and personal blogs.

The speaker emphasized that the **First Cloud AI Journey** program requires students to complete at least **5 side projects** that address the real "pain points" of 6 major industry groups.

#### Lessons Learned

The most important lesson from the session was the reality of the IT recruitment market:

> 90-100% of job opportunities at large organizations such as AWS come from internal postings or referrals, not public job channels.

This means that participating in communities and building professional relationships from an early stage is extremely important.

I also learned that combining a clear career direction with real, publicly shared side projects is the most effective way to stand out in the job market.

### 2. Real-World Experience of a Data Engineer

#### Speaker Information

The speaker of the session was **Anh Banh Cam Vinh**, who shared his practical experience in the **Data Engineering** field.

The presentation focused on the differences between the academic environment and the real working environment in technology companies.

#### Main Content

The speaker began by comparing how data is handled in university projects compared to real production systems.

In the academic environment, projects are often small and well-defined, while real systems must handle:

- Large volumes of transactions.
- Complex and changing data structures.
- Strict requirements for stability and growth.
- Trade-offs between technical quality and cost/time constraints.

The speaker emphasized that data engineers are responsible for processing large and complex transactions while ensuring the system grows stably over time.

He also shared important lessons about problem-solving in the workplace:

- Understanding the business requirements behind technical tasks.
- Balancing technical solutions with cost and time.
- Looking beyond immediate requirements to anticipate future system growth, which is called an **outcome-oriented mindset**.
- Understanding why a technical requirement is necessary for the business.

The speaker also discussed the importance of a solid technical foundation, including:

- Networking.
- IT Security.
- Cloud fundamentals.

These subjects, which are taught at university, are the foundation that helps engineers solve real-world problems effectively.

The presentation also introduced tools and practices used in production environments, such as using AI-powered tools to scan, evaluate, and improve the quality of personal projects so that they approach real production standards.

#### Lessons Learned

The lesson I learned was that real work is very different from studying at university.

In the workplace, solving a problem is not only about writing correct code. It also requires understanding the business, balancing resources, and thinking ahead about how the system will grow.

I also realized the importance of building a strong foundation in Networking, IT Security, and Cloud during my studies. These fundamentals make it much easier to learn and work in real projects later.

### 3. Soft Skills and Communication: Facing Fears at Work

#### Speaker Information

The speaker of the session was **Chi Nhu Tran** (from AWS), who guided the students in the topic of psychology and overcoming fears when starting a career.

The presentation focused on communication skills, dealing with anxiety, and building professional relationships in the workplace.

#### Main Content

The speaker first addressed the fear that many students and new graduates face when entering the workplace.

She led an activity in which students closed their eyes and acknowledged their personal fears. This exercise helped relieve **peer pressure** and created an open space for young people to step out of their comfort zone.

The presentation then covered practical communication skills, including:

- How to communicate with superiors and colleagues.
- How to handle real workplace situations.
- How to build professional working relationships.
- How to start a conversation, which is often called **small talk**.

The speaker performed a live situational exercise on how to begin a conversation with a stranger or a manager to build a professional network.

Another memorable part of the session was a humorous but practical motto shared by the speaker:

> Treat your boss and customers like "loved ones" to achieve the highest work efficiency.

This means understanding their needs, communicating with empathy, and building trust rather than just completing tasks mechanically.

The speaker also shared advice on career management:

- Facing the fear of failure.
- Being persistent when applying for jobs.
- Continuing to learn continuously.
- Building confidence through preparation and recognizing small achievements.

#### Lessons Learned

The lesson I learned was that soft skills are just as important as technical skills in the workplace.

Communication, networking, and the ability to face fears are essential for a successful career, regardless of technical expertise.

I also realized that acknowledging fears and stepping out of the comfort zone, even in small steps, is the way to build confidence and grow professionally.

### 4. Learning Mindset and Using AI Effectively

#### Speaker Information

The speaker of the session was **Anh Khang**, a Swinburne alumni, who shared his learning journey, how he applied AI in his studies, and his work experience.

The presentation focused on building a strong foundation, using AI correctly, and developing an outcome-oriented way of thinking.

#### Main Content

The speaker emphasized the importance of a solid foundation in learning. According to him, students should study the foundational subjects deeply, including:

- Networking.
- IT Security.
- Cloud.

He warned about a common mistake among students: letting AI do their homework for them. This leads to gaps in fundamental knowledge.

Instead, the speaker encouraged using AI as a support tool for thinking:

- Use AI to ask the right questions.
- Use AI to understand the essence of problems deeply.
- Use AI to validate information rather than accepting it blindly.
- Apply **prompt engineering** to interact with AI effectively.

The speaker also shared his personal learning experience:

- Using AI tools such as ChatGPT and LLMs to support learning.
- Focusing on understanding concepts rather than memorizing answers.
- Building projects to apply knowledge in practice.
- Adopting an **outcome-oriented mindset**, looking beyond immediate requirements to anticipate future needs.

He encouraged students to improve their old projects even after submission, even if they are not graded, to make their capability profiles stand out in interviews.

The speaker also discussed the importance of treating learning and work with a long-term perspective, continuously improving and building a strong personal brand.

#### Lessons Learned

The lesson I learned was that AI is a powerful tool, but it must be used correctly.

Using AI to think and ask questions is much more valuable than letting AI do the work for us.

I also realized the importance of building a strong foundation and continuously improving my projects and skills. An **outcome-oriented mindset** and a public capability profile are key factors for standing out in the job market.

### 5. Networking, Community and Career Opportunities

#### Session Content

The sharing session was also an opportunity for networking and career orientation, with the participation of a delegation of students from **Swinburne Can Tho**.

The program emphasized the importance of technology communities in career development:

- **AWS Study Group** – a community for learning and exchanging AWS knowledge.
- **First Cloud AI Journey** – a program focused on building future engineers in Vietnam.

According to the speakers, participating in these communities helps students:

- Access internal job postings and referrals.
- Learn from experienced engineers and alumni.
- Build a network of professional relationships.
- Stay updated with the latest cloud and AI trends.

#### Applying to Work

The session reminded me that studying and working in the technology field should not be done alone. Joining communities and building relationships are important steps to unlock career opportunities.

I plan to:

- Actively participate in the **AWS Study Group** and **First Cloud AI Journey** communities.
- Connect with alumni and engineers to learn from their experiences.
- Build and publicly share real side projects to build my capability profile.
- Continuously improve my skills in Cloud, Data, and AI.

#### Lessons Learned

The lesson I learned was that networking and community participation are not optional in the technology field. They are essential for accessing the best career opportunities, especially at large organizations where most positions are filled through internal referrals.

Combining technical skills, soft skills, and community participation is the key to a sustainable and successful career in IT.
