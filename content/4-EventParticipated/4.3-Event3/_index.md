## Summary of the Event and Lessons Learned

### 1. Overview of FCAJ - AGENTIC AI BUILD WEEK

#### Program Information

> **FCAJ - AGENTIC AI BUILD WEEK** is a program for people who are interested in artificial intelligence, particularly Agentic AI. The event provides participants with an opportunity to learn about, build, and develop AI solutions in a practical, creative, and collaborative environment.

#### Main Content

The event was centered around applying **Agentic AI** to real-world problems.

Unlike conventional chatbots that mainly respond to user inputs, AI agents can take in a goal, analyze the task, build a plan, and execute multiple steps to reach the desired outcome.

An AI agent is capable of doing things such as:

* Receiving and understanding user requests.
* Breaking a big goal into smaller tasks.
* Creating a step-by-step action plan.
* Using external tools or APIs.
* Fetching and processing data.
* Checking results and adjusting its actions.
* Working together with other AI agents to finish tasks.

During the event, I had the chance to watch how participating teams came up with ideas, picked their problems, built prototypes, and presented their final solutions.

#### Lessons Learned

I now understand Agentic AI much better and can clearly tell the difference between AI agents and regular chatbots.

A good Agentic AI product needs more than just a powerful model. It also requires a clear objective, a logical workflow, good data, and the ability to connect with outside tools.

### 2. Benefits of Participating in a Hackathon

#### Main Content

A hackathon is an event where participants work alone or in teams to build a product within a limited amount of time.

Taking part in a hackathon brings many benefits for knowledge, skills, and career development.

First of all, a hackathon gives participants the chance to put theory into practice. Instead of learning technology only from documents and tutorials, participants have to analyze a real problem, choose a solution, and build a working prototype.

Hackathons also sharpen problem-solving skills. Within a short time, teams must decide which requirements matter most, prioritize features, and deal with problems that come up while building.

Participants can also grow skills such as:

* Analyzing and identifying problems.
* Generating creative ideas.
* Planning the implementation.
* Working effectively in a team.
* Dividing responsibilities.
* Managing time.
* Presenting and defending ideas.
* Taking feedback from experts.
* Building a working prototype quickly.

#### Lessons Learned

A hackathon is more than just a coding competition. It is also a place where participants can learn, experiment, and grow.

The most valuable result of a hackathon is not necessarily winning a prize. The whole process of building a product, working with teammates, and solving real problems is already a valuable experience in itself.

---

### 3. Developing Teamwork Skills

#### Main Content

Most hackathon projects are done by teams. Each member usually takes on a different role, such as frontend development, backend development, AI, UI design, business analysis, or product presentation.

To finish a product within a short time, team members must agree on common goals, keep communicating, and coordinate their work well.

A hackathon team typically needs to:

* Define the common goals of the project.
* Pick an idea that fits the event theme.
* Assign tasks based on each member's strengths.
* Track the progress of the work.
* Help members who run into problems.
* Combine the different parts into one complete product.
* Prepare the presentation and demo.

If the team does not communicate well, they may end up with duplicated work, missing features, or difficulty integrating the product before the deadline.

#### Lessons Learned

Teamwork plays a very important role in a hackathon.

A team can have a great idea but still fail if the members do not work well together. On the other hand, a team that divides work properly and supports each other can achieve good results in a short time.

### 4. Improving Time and Product Scope Management

#### Main Content

Hackathons usually limit the time available for development. So participants cannot build every feature from the start. They need to focus on the most important parts of their solution.

Teams often build their product as a **Minimum Viable Product – MVP**. An MVP is a version of the product that includes enough core features to prove that the idea can work.

The MVP building process may include:

1. Identifying the problem to solve.
2. Defining the target users.
3. Proposing a solution.
4. Choosing the most important features.
5. Building a prototype or experimental product.
6. Testing the main user flow.
7. Preparing the demo.
8. Presenting the value of the product.

Managing the scope well helps teams focus on what matters and avoid spending too much time on unnecessary details.

#### Lessons Learned

When time is limited, choosing the right priorities matters more than building too many features.

A demo product does not have to be as complete as a commercial product. But it must clearly show the problem, the solution, the target users, and the value it provides.

### 5. Expanding Knowledge of Agentic AI

#### Main Content

By following the event, I was introduced to many different possible uses of Agentic AI.

AI agents can be applied in areas such as:

* Customer support.
* Education and training.
* Data analysis.
* Task management.
* E-commerce.
* Finance.
* Healthcare.
* Cybersecurity.
* Software development.
* Business process automation.

An Agentic AI system may consist of the following components:

* **Large Language Model:** Understands requests and creates responses.
* **Memory:** Stores information and interaction history.
* **Planning:** Makes plans to reach the assigned goals.
* **Tools:** Connects to APIs, databases, or external services.
* **Knowledge Base:** Supplies relevant data and knowledge to the AI agent.
* **Evaluation:** Checks the quality of the output.
* **Guardrails:** Controls the system's behavior and ensures safety.

Combining these parts allows AI agents to handle much more complex tasks than regular chatbots.

#### Lessons Learned

When building an AI agent, developers need to think about the whole system workflow, not just the language model.

Things like data quality, tool usage, memory management, security, and result evaluation all have a big impact on how reliable an Agentic AI product is.

### 6. Developing Creative and Problem-Solving Thinking

#### Main Content

Hackathons push participants to find real problems and come up with fresh solutions.

A good idea does not have to use the most complex technology. It just needs to solve a specific problem and bring clear value to users.

While developing an idea, participants should think about questions such as:

* What is the current problem?
* Who is affected by it?
* What are the limits of existing solutions?
* How can Agentic AI help solve it?
* What benefits does the product bring?
* Is the solution practical?
* Can the product keep growing after the event?

This process helps participants build a user-centered mindset and avoid making a product just because a new technology exists.

#### Lessons Learned

Technology only becomes valuable when it is used to solve the right problem.

Before building a product, it is important to understand the users' needs, define the core value, and check whether the solution is feasible.

#### Main Content

Participants may get the chance to meet:

* Students from different universities.
* Software developers.
* Designers.

Networking is one of the most valuable parts of attending a tech event.

By actively talking, asking questions, and sharing ideas, participants learn more and also build relationships that can support their studies and career.

### 8. Improving Product Presentation Skills

#### Main Content

Besides building a product, participating teams also have to present their solutions within a limited time.

A hackathon presentation usually includes:

* The problem to solve.
* The target users.
* The idea and solution.
* How Agentic AI is applied.
* The system architecture or workflow.
* The main features.
* A product demo.
* The practical value.
* Future expansion possibilities.

Presenters need to speak clearly, highlight the most important features, and help the audience understand the value of the solution.

A product can have great features, but a confusing presentation can make it hard for judges and attendees to see its strengths.

#### Lessons Learned

Presentation skills are just as important as development skills.

During a presentation, teams should focus on the problem, the value of the solution, and the demo, instead of only talking about the technologies used.
